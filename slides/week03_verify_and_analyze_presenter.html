Concept

What an estimate claims

A difference in means

The average of one group minus the average of the other.

A regression coefficient

With one yes-or-no predictor, the same difference, plus a standard way to say how uncertain it is.

An interval

A range that shows how much the estimate would move if we ran the study again.

Today's example, made up: a platform adds an “AI-generated” label to some posts. Does it change how likely people say they are to share them?

Concept

What an interval says, and what it does not

It says

If we ran the study many times, about 95 of 100 intervals built this way would contain the true effect.

It does not say

That the true effect has a 95% chance of being inside this one interval. Or that a result below .05 is a verdict.

A wider interval means more uncertainty. A bigger sample makes it narrower.

CACTUS Fall 2026 · Week 3

Concept

A confounder can flip the answer

Example

Students who attend tutoring score lower than students who do not.

The confounder

Students who were already struggling are more likely to attend tutoring.

What it does

The comparison mixes the effect of tutoring with who chooses tutoring.

An experiment avoids this by deciding who gets the treatment by chance. Survey or platform data usually cannot. You will see this in the coding session.

Writing it up

Over-claiming

A claim you will seeWhat it needs
“The label causes lower sharing.”A design that supports a cause, such as random assignment
“It is significant, so it is large.”Look at the size of the estimate, not only at the p-value
“No effect was found, so there is none.”A small sample may be too noisy to tell
“The AI said so.”A check you ran yourself

A notebook belongs in a repository

History

Keys, passwords, or private data. Anything you commit may be visible.
