# Week 7: Choose a track

**Theme:** One idea for everyone, four methods to choose from. The idea: **every method gets a known-answer test before you trust it on real data**, as in Weeks 3 and 6. Then each student picks the track that matches their project and runs a method on a dataset where the answer is planted, checks it, and applies it to their own data.
**Format:** Zoom. A 1-hour learning session for everyone (the idea, plus a short preview of each track), a 20-minute practice page, then a 2-hour coding session in **breakout rooms by track**, one facilitator hosting each room. About 3 hours live.

## Objectives

By the end of the week, students can:
1. Explain what a known-answer test is, and plant an answer in simulated data.
2. Run one method from their track and check it against the planted answer.
3. Say what the method assumes, and one way the real data will break that assumption.
4. Name what would make the result wrong (sampling, bots and duplicates, repeated people, small numbers).
5. Plan how to run the same method on their own data, and what to check first.

## The four tracks

Students choose one. Pairs form within a track. Choose by the end of Week 6 (tell the facilitators), so the rooms can be planned.

| Track | Good if your project is about | Method | Known answer planted in the data | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Networks** | Who talks to whom: replies, mentions, quotes, shared hashtags | Build a graph from edges, measure who is central, find communities | Two communities, a hub, and a few automated accounts | `networkx` |
| **B. Images and memes** | Repeated templates, reposts, visual spread | Fingerprint images (perceptual hash), group near-duplicates, count templates | Generated "meme" images from a handful of templates, with crops, captions, and noise | `Pillow`, `imagehash` |
| **C. Mock feed or experiment** | A randomized comparison: a source cue, a chatbot condition, a manipulated feed | Compare conditions, check the manipulation, estimate effects and an interaction | A planted effect and a planted interaction, with a failed manipulation check in one group | `statsmodels` |
| **D. Models for nested data** | Comments within threads or posts, people within groups, repeated measures | Regression that respects the grouping: standard errors that account for clusters, and a counts model with an exposure | Posts differ a lot, so ignoring them makes results look more certain than they are | `statsmodels` |

All data for the exercises is **invented** (generated by a script, so it is public and has a known answer). Students then use their own data for the homework.

## Between sessions (about 20 min): practice page

One practice page for everyone: **Plant It, Find It.** Three levels, then a bridge.

- **Plant an answer.** Choose how strong a planted effect (or structure) is, and see whether a simple method finds it. Make it too weak or the sample too small, and watch the method fail.
- **When structure is ignored.** The same data analysed two ways, with and without accounting for groups. See how the uncertainty changes.
- **What breaks the assumption.** Real data has bots, duplicates, and repeated people. Choose which one breaks which method.
- **Colab task:** plant a known answer in a few lines of code.

## Coding session (about 2 h): breakout rooms by track

One notebook per track, with the same skeleton, so a facilitator who knows one can help with another.

| Time | Exercise |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Warm-up:** plant a known answer in simulated data (a few lines, everyone). Predict what your method should find. |
| 0:15–0:45 | **Ex 1:** run your track's method on the planted data. |
| 0:45–1:10 | **Ex 2:** check it against the known answer: did it find what was planted, and how far off is it? Break the data on purpose (smaller sample, a bot, a duplicate) and watch the result move. |
| 1:10–1:35 | **Ex 3:** bug hunt. AI-written code for your track, with planted mistakes. Find them using the known answer. |
| 1:35–1:55 | **Ex 4:** your own data. List what you would check first, and run the first step on a sample of it. |
| 1:55–2:00 | Log; optional share-out |

Hints for Exercise 3 come from facilitators only.

## Homework (45 minutes or less)

- Run your track's method on your own data (or a sample), after one known-answer check.
- Write one paragraph: what the method assumes, and one way your data may break it.
- Start thinking about Week 8: what you will present, and what must rerun from scratch.

## Hand-off to Week 8

Students leave with a method that passed a known-answer check, and a plan for their own data. Week 8 is about rerunning the whole pipeline from scratch, documenting it, and presenting the results with their limits.

## Build plan (Week 7)

### Data generators (all invented, public, with a known answer)
| Track | Script | What it makes |
|---|---|---|
| A | `scripts/make_week07_network.py` | An edge list of replies and mentions for about 300 accounts: two planted communities, one hub, and a few bots that post to everyone. Known community labels |
| B | `scripts/make_week07_memes.py` | About 200 generated "meme" images from 6 templates, with crops, brightness changes, and caption variants, plus 40 unrelated images. Known template ids |
| C | `scripts/make_week07_experiment.py` | A 2 by 2 online experiment (about 400 people) with a manipulation check, an outcome, a planted main effect and a planted interaction, and one subgroup where the manipulation failed |
| D | `scripts/make_week07_nested.py` | About 3,000 comments in 80 posts: a binary outcome with a post-level effect and a comment-level predictor, and a count outcome with an exposure. Known coefficients and known between-post variation |

### Notebooks (one skeleton)
`notebooks/week07_track_a_networks.ipynb`, `..._b_images.ipynb`, `..._c_experiment.ipynb`, `..._d_nested.ipynb`. Each has: warm-up (plant an answer), Ex 1 (run the method), Ex 2 (check and break it), Ex 3 (bug hunt: three planted mistakes, in the private key), Ex 4 (own data). Checks use `assert` with helpful messages, tested with a correct solution as in Weeks 4 to 6.

Candidate mistakes (to choose three per track, private key):
- **A:** treating a reply as undirected when direction matters; counting bots as people; reading degree as influence without looking at who the neighbours are.
- **B:** hashing images at different sizes without resizing first; clustering with a threshold that merges different templates; calling a repost a new template.
- **C:** reporting a main effect when the interaction flips it; skipping the manipulation check; dropping failed-manipulation participants after seeing the result.
- **D:** ordinary standard errors on clustered data; treating a post-level predictor as a comment-level one; dropping the exposure from a counts model.

## Decisions and open items (Week 7)

2. **One facilitator per room.** With four facilitators and four tracks, the Desk would have to double as a room host. Alternative: the Desk hosts the smallest track.
3. **Pairs within a track.** Tracks with one student pair that student with a facilitator or a participant from a nearby track. Decide.
4. **Dates.** Week 7 is on Jan 12. Participants with earlier deadlines may need to do the work before then.
5. **Image libraries.** `imagehash` is not preinstalled in Colab (a short `pip install`). Confirm the install time.
6. **Using real data in the track notebooks.** None: all exercise data is invented. Students bring their own for Exercise 4 and the homework.
