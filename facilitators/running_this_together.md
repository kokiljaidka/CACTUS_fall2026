# Running CACTUS together

A short guide for the facilitators (RAs and PhD students) who run the sessions. If you are nervous about this, that is normal. The course is designed so that **no one person has to be the expert**.

> Items in [square brackets] are decisions Kokil will confirm. For roles, the rota, and how to run a session, start with the [facilitator hub](README.md).

## What this is

CACTUS is an eight-week, hands-on series where graduate students learn to use AI to write code for their own research, and to check that the code does what they meant. By the end each person has a finished project card, a working pipeline on their own data, and a record of how they checked it.

It is a shared learning community. The people running it are learning how to teach this, and the participants are learning how to do it. Everyone is expected to ask questions, and nobody is expected to know everything.

## What you are (and are not)

- You **are** a host and a guide. You keep the session on time, keep people unstuck, and keep the room kind.
- You are **not** the expert. The course's central skill is *checking*, so "I am not sure, let us test it" is exactly on message. You are demonstrating the skill, not failing at it.
- You are **not** alone. There is a script, a team, and a person on call (below).

## Roles in each session

Each session has a team, not one presenter. Roles rotate so everyone does each one (see the round-robin rota in the [facilitator hub](README.md)).

| Role | What they do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | Runs the one-hour learning session using the slides and script. In coding sessions, opens and closes and posts time checks. | About 1 hour of session plus about 1 hour of prep |
| **Desk** | Zoom host in the main room: breakout rooms, chat, "Ask for help", Colab and install problems, timekeeping, late joiners. | Session plus 15 minutes |
| **Rooms (everyone else)** | Visit breakout rooms. Ask guiding questions before giving answers. Use the hint ladder in the hub and the answer key. | Session only |
| **Data helper** (Weeks 1 to 4) | Helps students who do not have data yet get an export from Twitter/X, Instagram, Facebook, Reddit, or Bluesky (talk offline in Week 1), writes down what worked in that row of the access guide with the date, and helps at the optional access clinic. | About 1 to 2 hours in total |
| **On call** | Kokil, reachable during every session for anything the team cannot resolve. | [Kokil to confirm availability] |

Suggested weekly rhythm for the team:

1. **Before** (about 45 minutes, together or alone): read the week's script, do the practice page and notebook yourself, note anything you would stumble on.
2. **During** (about 3 hours across the two sessions, from Week 2; Week 1 is about 4 hours).
3. **After** (15 minutes): post three lines in the team chat: what worked, what stalled, what to fix. Anything that was confusing becomes a GitHub issue so the next cohort benefits.

## When you do not know

You will get a question you cannot answer. Here is what to say:

- "Good question. Let us test it." (then run it, or ask the AI and check the answer together)
- "I do not know. I will find out and post it in the chat." (then do, within a day)
- "Let me bring in Kokil on that one." (post it in the team chat; see below)

**Escalation, in order:**

1. Ask your co-facilitator in the team chat (about five minutes).
2. If still stuck, post the question in the team chat using three lines: *where, what you tried, what you expected*. Kokil or another team member replies.
3. If the participant is blocked and it will take longer, offer them the saved-results route or a partner, and follow up afterwards.

**Never** paste a participant's data, API key, or personal information into an AI chat or a public issue.

## Making it a community, not a class

- **Pairs and partners** in every coding session. People help each other first, and facilitators second.
- **A shared place to ask** [GitHub Discussions, or a chat group: Kokil to choose]. Participants answer each other, and facilitators answer what is left.
- **Show and tell** at the end of each session: two or three volunteers share a card, a chart, or a bug they found. This makes learning visible and normal.
- **Everyone can improve the materials.** Typos, unclear instructions, and broken links can be fixed with a GitHub issue or a pull request. Contributors are listed on the course site.
- **Bring in alumni and other PhD students** as guest floaters for a session, so the load is shared.
- **Norms for the room:** be kind, assume good intent, no shaming for not knowing, and keep participants' data private.

## Questions you might be asking (and honest answers)

**Why are we doing this?**
So that the people in our group learn to use AI for research responsibly, finish a real project, and so that there is a reusable course for others. It is not meant as busywork.

**Who is in charge?**
Kokil is accountable for the course, and is on call. The facilitators run the sessions as a team, using the script. Nobody is "in charge" of you; the materials are.

**Am I being paid, or credited?**
[Kokil to answer this clearly before the first rehearsal.]

**What if I mess up?**
Everyone does. The course runs on the idea that mistakes are things you check and fix. A session that goes wrong is a note for the next version.

**What if nobody turns up?**
Then it is a quiet session and you have time to improve the materials. Tell the team.

**What if I get a question about the research itself?**
You are not expected to advise on their research. Say: "That is a great question for your supervisor or for the next session. Let us note it." Keep to the course content.
